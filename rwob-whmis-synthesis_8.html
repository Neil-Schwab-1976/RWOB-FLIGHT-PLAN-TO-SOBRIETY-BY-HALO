Section 1

The Unifying Insight

The Workplace Hazardous Materials Information System has protected Canadian workers since 1988 using a colour-coded safety recognition system that every certified worker already understands. The foundational insight behind this framework: the colours that keep a worker safe from hazardous materials are the same colours that can describe the hazardous conditions within a person.

The planet needs restoration from WHITE through GOLD. The human needs restoration from WHITE through GOLD. The work is the same work. This "same-denominator principle" means a single colour progression governs outer ecological restoration and inner psychological restoration — and, critically for Recovery Without Borders, governs the routing of people to the treatment services that match their actual needs.

Why this matters for addiction services: Currently, intake systems route clients to treatment based on plain-language need categories, waitlist availability, or geographic proximity. None of these methods account for the emotional, neurochemical, or archetypical profile of the client's condition — or match it to the corresponding strengths of the treating organization. The WHMIS colour system provides that standardized matching language, built on recognition symbols 38 years of Canadian workers already carry. Recovery Without Borders (RWOB) is one proposed application — an online intake system that would use this colour-match architecture to route clients across 67+ Ontario organizations.

The colour-match system operates in two directions simultaneously. Side A maps the client's identified stressors to WHMIS colour attributes — neurochemical, emotional, and archetypical. Blend Logic

Many organizations handle multiple colour domains. The tracking sheet records these as blends — e.g., "BLUE + BLACK" for an organization treating substance use with concurrent mental health in residential/crisis settings. Trafalgar Addiction Treatment Centres, for example, carries BLUE (health hazard / depth / oxytocin-connection work) + BLACK (gravity / transformation / full neurochemical activation) because their modalities span both domains.

Dual-Track Stressor Architecture

The stressor architecture operates on two completely separate tracks feeding the same Human Value Function (W) from opposite directions. Track A identifies what destabilizes a participant's W; Track B identifies what destabilizes a worker's W. Both tracks are academically validated — Track A against peer-reviewed clinical literature (Gorski, Marlatt & Donovan, PAWS neuroscience), Track B through the Lighthouse Resilience Program (scored 100% at triOS College).

W = (R + B + N + V + M + Gv + Fe + Fm) × Cs

Section 10

The Case for Standardization

The proposal to the Health & Safety Board / WHMIS Oversight Committee rests on four amendments. The first two are the primary requests and stand independently; the fluorescent proposals are parallel technical amendments.

Amendment Substance Status
1. GREEN — New Category Official WHMIS hazard category for psychological and emotional hazards: cumulative stress, vicarious trauma, compassion fatigue, moral injury. Anchored to CCOHS evidence and CSA Z1003 (National Standard for Psychological Health and Safety in the Workplace). Prepared for submission — not yet submitted
2. BLACK — Expanded Formal recognition of BLACK as both biohazard/warning colour and the psychological shadow layer — the long-term chronic and archetypal weight of hazardous work. Prepared for submission — not yet submitted
3. Fluorescent Orange Standard text/symbol colour across all earthbound WHMIS labels for improved recognition under low-light and high-stress conditions. Parallel technical amendment
4. Fluorescent Yellow Reserved for BLACK label on space/orbital operations only — the Black Yellow Label. Parallel technical amendment
The addiction services angle: Chapter 6 of the Canadian Health and Safety Workplace Fundamentals textbook already explicitly addresses psycho-social hazards educationally. The regulatory system must match what the educational system already recognizes. Workers in addiction services — who face vicarious trauma, compassion fatigue, and moral injury daily — carry occupational hazards that the current WHMIS system does not name. GREEN names them. BLACK names the long-term weight of carrying them. Once named in the regulatory system, support infrastructure can be built to match the burden — and the same colour vocabulary that protects the worker also routes the client to the right treatment.
