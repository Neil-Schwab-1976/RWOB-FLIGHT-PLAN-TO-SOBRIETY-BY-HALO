Synopsis

Flight Plan to Sobriety is a free, self-guided onboarding page for Recovery Without Borders, built by HALO Antifragile AI. A person seeking help answers questions privately, at their own pace, in any language their browser translates to. They leave with a personal Flight Plan, and RWOB receives their intake by email so a worker can call and make a warm introduction to a matched clinician or program nearby.

The page has two sides:

  • Member side: a safety gate, a short practical snapshot (RAFT), targeted screens, a needs check-in mapped to the HALO-WBBRY colour key, ten "how you like to work" questions (OCEAN), then matched services and clinicians, a Flight Plan with a dated Future Ripple, and a Send button.
  • Clinician side: clinics and clinicians list themselves: postal code, services, how they work, treatment length and wait time. Their services are coded to the same colour key, so the match runs on one shared language.

The match weighs how well a clinician's ways of working fit the person's answers, how close they are, and colour fit. Long waits lower the score a little. The person always chooses; nothing is assigned.

How it works: member side

The person moves through nine steps. Only the safety gate must come before anything else; everything after it can be skipped or left blank.

  1. Profile. Name they go by and the first three characters of their postal code. Phone and email are optional and only needed if they want a call.
  2. Safety gate. Nine yes/no questions modelled on the C-SSRS: suicidal thoughts, plan, past attempt, harm to others, dangerous withdrawal, urgent medical need, psychosis, being unsafe tonight. Any "yes" opens a crisis screen with 9-8-8, text CONNECT to 686868, 9-1-1 and NEDIC. They can choose to continue.
  3. RAFT snapshot. Roof tonight, any urgent medical or withdrawal concern, basic needs, and in their own words what they need most.
  4. Targeted screens. Open only when RAFT points to them: CAGE-AID, SCOFF, PHQ-2, GAD-2, PC-PTSD-5 and a brief gambling screen. Results are flags for a worker, never a diagnosis.
  5. Check-in and "how you like to work." They name where they want to go and be, rate 26 statements about the past two weeks, and answer 10 optional OCEAN questions.
  6. Profile and matches. Their HALO-WBBRY colour profile, two RWOB pathways, colour-matched services from 74 sample Ontario organizations, and clinicians ranked by fit, distance and availability. They tick what they want.
  7. Use and HALT. Optional: main substance, years, days since last use, past attempts, overdose history, medications, and four HALT sliders. Stopping alcohol or benzodiazepines in the first week triggers a medical-safety warning.
  8. Hurdles and 72-hour plan. The likely PAWS hurdles for their profile are shown and they tick the ones that feel true. Then four lines in their own words: one thing tomorrow, who they'll call, next appointment, one thing different in three days.
  9. Flight Plan and Send. One page to print or save: hurdles, matched G.A.P.S. tools, lanes, services, Future Ripple, check-in dates and crisis lines. Send emails everything to RWOB, only after they tick consent.

How services are colour-coded

  • WHITE Navigation, harm reduction, housing stabilization, primary care, information-only services
  • BLUE Community counselling, outpatient, family or couples therapy, grief work, concurrent-disorder counselling, 2SLGBTQ2S+-affirming care
  • YELLOW Crisis support, withdrawal management, RAAM clinics, problem gambling
  • BLACK Residential, hospital or inpatient care, intensive addiction medicine, trauma or PTSD programs, long-term recovery, eating disorder specialty
  • RED Never matched to a listing. Anyone in danger is sent to 9-8-8 or 9-1-1.

How the match works

Both sides speak the same HALO-WBBRY colour key, so a member's needs and a clinician's services meet in the middle. Every match card shows what it matched on, so a worker can check it.

1. The member's colour: what they need

The 26 check-in statements score White, Blue, Yellow and Black. Red comes only from the safety gate and RAFT, never from statements. The highest colour is primary; a second above 25% is secondary.

2. How they like to work: OCEAN

Ten optional questions shape which ways of working rise, never the colour. Comfortable sharing raises group options; slow to trust raises one-to-one first. Liking clear steps raises structured approaches (CBT); liking new things raises flexible ones (ACT). Feelings that often overwhelm raise emotion-regulation skills (DBT). Valuing connection raises relational, talk-it-through workers.

A clinician must cover at least one real need beyond motivational interviewing to appear at all.

4. RWOB's own pathways

Two options at most, from the CEO's routing matrix. A positive mood, anxiety, trauma or eating screen puts Free Therapy Referral first; a substance concern or Yellow profile puts the 24/7 Digital App first; Blue puts Peer Support Groups first; otherwise Weekly City Forums. Each comes with its matrix second choice.

5. Services directory

74 sample Ontario organizations, colour-coded from public information and not yet confirmed by them, ranked by colour fit with a boost for the member's region.

User guide: people seeking support

It takes about 15 to 25 minutes, it's free, and you can stop at any point. There are no right or wrong answers; you are the expert on your own life.

  1. Open the link on a phone, tablet or computer. To read it in another language, use your browser's Translate (in Chrome, right-click the page and choose "Translate").
  2. Make your profile. Type the name you go by and the first three characters of your postal code. Add a phone or email only if you want someone to reach you; otherwise press Continue without contact details.
  3. Answer the safety questions honestly. A "yes" isn't a judgment. It shows you who to call right now, and you can still carry on.
  4. Fill in the quick snapshot: roof tonight, any urgent medical concern, what you need today, and in your own words what matters most.
  5. Answer any short follow-up questions that open. They help a worker know what to prioritize.
  6. Say where you want to go and be, then rate the 26 statements. Open each coloured section to answer it. The 10 "how you like to work" questions are optional but make your match better.
  7. Look at your results: your colour profile, two Recovery Without Borders options, and services and clinicians near you. Tick any you'd like to move forward with. Nothing is final until you choose.
  8. Build your Flight Plan (optional, about 10 minutes): a little about your use, the hurdles that feel true for you, and your next 72 hours in your own words.
  9. Keep it. Press Print or save as PDF, or Copy as text. The page keeps nothing once you close it.
  10. Send it to RWOB if you want a call. Tick the agreement box and press Send to Recovery Without Borders. A worker can then introduce you to the service you chose, so you don't have to tell your story again.
If it gets to be too much at any point: call or text 9-8-8 (24/7), text CONNECT to 686868, or call 9-1-1 in an emergency.

User guide: clinicians and programs

Listing takes about five minutes. People are matched to you by what they need, the ways you work, how close you are and how soon you can see them.

  1. Open the link and press I'm a clinician or program at the top.
  2. Enter who and where: your name or the program's, your role, organization, an email for referrals, and the postal code where you see people. Choose in person, virtual, or both. Virtual-only listings don't need a postal code.
  3. Tick the services you actually provide. Your colour code builds as you tick. Members whose profile leans those colours see you higher.
  4. Describe your treatment in your own words. RWOB reads this to check your colour mapping before a real listing goes live.
  5. Add the typical length of treatment (or tick "ongoing") and your usual wait for a first appointment. Members see both, and their Future Ripple uses them.
  6. Tick how you work. This is the biggest part of the match, so tick only what you offer.
  7. Add who you specialise in, your cost and whether you're taking new people. "Not right now" keeps you out of matching.
  8. Agree to be listed and press Add me to the list.

Members see your name, role, area, distance, how you see people, treatment length, wait, colour code and the ways of working that matched them. You receive a member's details only when they choose you and agree to share them. In this demo a listing lasts only in that browser tab; the Copy the list for the RWOB team box holds it in a form RWOB can load into its own system.

User guide: RWOB staff

A finished intake arrives at neil.s@recoverywithoutborders.org, copied to toronto@recoverywithoutborders.org, once the person ticks consent and presses Send. The worker reads it, calls, and makes the warm introduction.

One-time setup, before the first demo

  • Complete one intake with made-up details and press Send. It will say it didn't send because the form "needs activation." That is expected.
  • Open the activation email from FormSubmit in neil.s@ (check spam) and click the link.
  • Send once more and confirm it lands in neil.s@ with toronto@ copied.
  • Optional: swap the visible address for the alias FormSubmit provides.

What the email contains

  1. Subject "New intake — Flight Plan to Sobriety — [name]", with SAFETY FLAG, CALL FIRST added if any safety question was answered yes.
  2. Contact details, how and when they want to be reached, and whether it's safe to leave a message.
  3. Intake summary: colour profile, four wellbeing areas, suggested RWOB pathway, their own words, roof, substance, basic needs, screen results.
  4. Staff flags: safety gate, housing, colour, positive screens, "live call only."
  5. Services and clinicians they chose.
  6. The G.A.P.S. onboarding record: PFD snapshot, substance profile, HALT baseline, PAWS position, hurdles picked, tools loaded, 72-hour plan, how they like to work, Future Ripple, check-in dates, and a one-line encounter prompt.
  7. Their whole Flight Plan, exactly as they see it.

Suggested call: open with their own words from RAFT, confirm the safety flags, offer the one or two choices they ticked, and book the Day 1 and Day 3 check-ins from their Flight Plan.

Five-minute walkthrough for leadership

  1. Open the demo and point out the banner, "Free" and the translate line.
  2. Make a profile with made-up details and answer "no" to the safety questions. Go back, answer one "yes", and show the crisis screen.
  3. Pick alcohol in RAFT, answer the screens, rate the check-in, and answer the OCEAN questions two different ways to show the match shift.
  4. Show results: colour, RWOB pathways, services, clinicians and what each matched on.
  5. Switch to "I'm a clinician or program", list a Newmarket clinic, return to the member side, and show it ranking near the top at under 1 km.
  6. Build the Flight Plan, show the Future Ripple, then press Send and open the email.

Privacy, safety and current limits

The demo is safe to show with made-up details. It is not ready for real clients until the items below are resolved with RWOB leadership and its clinical and legal advisors.

  • Nothing is saved on the device or a server. Closing the page clears everything.
  • Nothing is sent unless the person ticks consent and presses Send. The intake then goes by email through FormSubmit, a third-party US service: acceptable for made-up demo data only, not real health information.
  • The page cannot contact anyone in a crisis. It shows 9-8-8, 686868, 9-1-1 and NEDIC instead, and says so plainly.

Safety built in: the safety gate runs first; any "yes" shows crisis lines before anything else; answering "Hopeless" about the future shows 9-8-8 on the spot; stopping alcohol or benzodiazepines in the first week triggers a seizure-risk warning; screens are labelled as flags, not diagnoses; nothing claims a worker has been notified.

What's next and version history

The next real step is a server behind the page. One decision unlocks shared listings, remembered login and real emails to every party: whether RWOB's GoHighLevel can receive intakes and listings by webhook.

  • RWOB leadership decides where intakes and listings are stored
  • Privacy and consent review of what is collected, sent and stored
  • Clinical advisor reviews the screens, colour mapping, PAWS weights and OCEAN rules
  • Invite the first real clinics to list themselves
  • Human-checked translations of the safety gate and crisis screen
